# Research prompt — Courses › Objectives / Outcomes / Requirements

Using Mobbin & Refero MCP, research how top apps design their **course objectives, learning outcomes and requirements** (the "what will learners achieve / what do they need before starting" layer of a course). Find the most relevant screens and user flows, compare patterns across products, and identify what the best apps do differently. Create a visual HTML report (images embedded directly) showing the top examples, common patterns, and the best practices top companies converge on. Keep the big picture in mind: this is one sub-module of **managing courses** in Essentials 2.0 (Semester → Courses → this).

## What the instructor is trying to do here
Define, for a course, (a) learning objectives / outcomes learners should reach, (b) prerequisites or requirements (prior courses, skills, equipment, attendance), and (c) how these show up to the learner and later feed assessment. In simulation education, objectives often map to competencies (see sub-module 03) and to scenarios (05) — note the seam but do not research those.

- "course prerequisites settings with required prior courses selector"

- "curriculum editor with objectives mapped to lessons or modules"

- "editing course requirements and publishing the course"
Refero: same intents; also "goal setting" and "OKR / objectives editor" in Lattice, Notion, Asana Goals, Linear projects for the generic "define measurable outcome" pattern.
Products to prioritise: Coursera (what you'll learn / skills you'll gain), Udemy course creation (objectives + requirements are mandatory fields), Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi, Canvas (outcomes), Moodle (competencies/completion), Docebo, 360Learning, Maven; generic: Asana Goals, Lattice, Notion.

## Questions the board answers (Q1–Q6: these become the H2 sections of the report and the columns of the comparison table)
1. Objectives are structured (list items, not free text) and reorderable
2. Objectives are visible to the learner in the same shape the instructor authored them
3. Requirements / prerequisites are enforced or only informational (and how that's communicated)
4. Objectives can be linked downstream (lesson, assessment, competency)
5. Guidance for authoring (examples, verb prompts, character limits, AI suggestions)
6. Where in the course-creation flow this step sits (required up front vs. optional later)

# Q5 — Guidance for authoring

Udemy provides the strongest course-specific constraint. Its **Intended learners** step says authors must enter at least four “learning objectives or outcomes,” uses one field per outcome, and shows a number at the right of every field. The displayed values (for example, 140 remaining after “Basics of journaling”) imply a 160-character limit rather than a live character count. It explains that these statements appear publicly and affect course performance. The prerequisites field supplies a concrete placeholder—“No programming experience needed. You will learn everything you need to know”—but this models readiness copy, not a measurable outcome. No outcome example, action-verb bank, measurability check, or AI suggestion is visible. [mobbin:3558619c-01e4-44de-9e1b-159104717ba1] [mobbin:3476c06a-c7b3-49ac-bc99-6875a0563ec6]

Asana offers a transferable guidance pattern. Onboarding asks for a main objective through three labeled choices, each paired with a short concrete explanation, while preserving “I’m not sure yet.” [refero:195b0110-a6d4-404f-81bf-be74a9af62c1] Its goal flow is closer to measurable authoring: the empty description prompts “Why is it important? How will you define success?”, offers **Improve your goal with Asana intelligence**, and shows progress as a percentage. The preview sequence is: (1) choose **Connect a project**, (2) search/select “Design Project,” (3) see one connected project and automatic progress. This suggests AI and downstream evidence can sit beside success criteria, though it does not show the AI’s output or learning-objective verbs. [mobbin:2f67fe8d-dbad-4ee4-ad75-5d08c69fe948]

Kajabi’s focused prompt generates a course outline from “Wireframing for beginners,” then lets the author review/edit modules, lessons, and descriptions and **Apply** or **Discard** them. This is strong author control over AI structure, but it does not guide measurable learning objectives. [mobbin:deee3b15-0748-485c-9921-7be6cc0a456a] [mobbin:4753100b-9870-45a7-88f7-447b3760e712] Its Ama suggestions generate opt-in page copy, headlines, or sales email, so they are marketing guidance rather than objective guidance. [mobbin:5e554c6d-92e6-4852-99e2-f863def8f299]

Teachable shows an AI assistant beside lesson content, but the captures do not reveal prompts or generated objective guidance; the second screen only shows ordinary selected-text formatting. [mobbin:c9d90a3c-c778-4949-ad9f-5537e5caf0a7] [mobbin:ffec1858-d352-4d00-9f52-585ce701769f]

Canva’s generic Magic Write prompt and social-caption output are too distant from course objectives and are rejected. No evidence shows Bloom-style verb prompts, measurable-outcome examples, or an AI measurability critique.

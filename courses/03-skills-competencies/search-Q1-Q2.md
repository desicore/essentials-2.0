# Mobbin Q1–Q2 search log

Task intent for every call: Research how top apps design skills-competencies for course management in an education platform.

## Coverage

Kept 7 references across 7 products: Lyssna, Maze, Optimal Workshop, ClassDojo, Coursera, Codecademy, and Teachable. The strongest direct evidence is Coursera’s “Skills you’ll gain” tag row, Codecademy’s subject/language inventory with progress bars, and ClassDojo’s reusable skills tiles with positive/needs-work states. Lyssna, Maze, and Optimal Workshop are honest adjacent tree editors showing expand/collapse, nested nodes, drag/reorder, bulk import/export, and keyboard navigation. Teachable is an adjacent five-step curriculum editing flow with checkboxes and bulk actions.

Named Canvas, Moodle, Lattice, Workday Skills Cloud, LinkedIn Learning, and Degreed did not appear in these Mobbin results. No evidence was promoted from the iOS searches because results were generic marketplace, social, or course-view screens rather than instructor taxonomy management.

## Q1/Q2 synthesis

Evidence favors a central taxonomy with local selection: hierarchical trees for authoring and bulk operations, paired with searchable/tagged skill chips for course views. For hundreds of items, expose search and filters alongside expand/collapse, preserve parent context, support multi-select, and provide bulk import/export. Free-form tags alone lack stable reuse and traceability; tiles and chips work best as the learner-facing or course-facing projection of canonical competencies.
